Extension to indels (exploratory, partial sample) — following the "continue with indels" request
================================================================================================================

METHOD
-------
Until now, exact-allele matching completely excluded indels (a limitation
documented from the start: dbSNP's SPDI anchoring convention does not
correspond directly to VCF's base-anchored convention).

Implemented a normalization function (scripts/normalize.py): trim the
shared suffix then the shared prefix between REF and ALT (VCF), to
obtain a minimal form directly comparable to dbSNP's SPDI format
(verified: idempotent on an already-minimal SPDI-style input). Self-tests
included in the script, all pass.

Accepted and now QUANTIFIED (not just assumed) limitation: this is a
simple prefix/suffix trim, not a full realignment against the reference
sequence in homopolymer/short-repeat regions -- two tools may choose
different but equivalent anchorings for the same indel in such a
context, and this script will not make them match.

RESULT ON THE PARTIAL SAMPLE (400MB, not full coverage)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Candidate panel extended to indels: 55,299 (of which 7,451 indels), versus
47,841 SNVs alone -- i.e. +13.5% additional real candidates.

Extended 2014-2017-merge subset: 4,096 candidates (1,439 indels, 2,657
SNVs) versus 2,657 before (SNVs only).

MATCH SUCCESS RATE, indels vs SNVs (dbSNP138 bundle):
  SNV   : 2650/2657 (99.7%) find a match in the bundle
  Indel : 1223/1439 (85.0%) find a match -- the remainder (15%)
          splits into "absent" (79, 5.5%) and mostly **"neither current nor
          stale" (137, 9.5%)** -- this last category is **100%
          composed of indels (0 SNVs)**, precisely and
          empirically confirming the normalization limitation documented above.

STALE RATE AMONG ACTUALLY-MATCHED VARIANTS (partial sample):
                          Bundle 138 (2014)         Bundle 144 (June 2015)
SNV  -- stale              219/2650 = 8.26%          18/2627 = 0.69%
Indel -- stale               14/1223 = 1.14%          12/1205 = 1.00%
SNV  -- ambiguous             0/2650 = 0.00%          60/2627 = 2.28%
Indel -- ambiguous           49/1223 = 4.01%         140/1205 = 11.62%

CONCLUSION -- a real contrast, not just "indels confirm the same thing"
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Indels are LESS often stale than SNVs (1.0-1.1% vs 0.7-8.3%)
   -- plausible direction: indels in difficult regions are
   perhaps less often duplicate submissions (submitted less often
   in duplicate by automated pipelines), or "successful matching" already
   filters for the simplest/non-repetitive indels, which may also be
   the least prone to duplicates -- not yet elucidated, to investigate.
2. Indels are MUCH more often AMBIGUOUS (4-12% vs 0-2.3% for
   SNVs) -- consistent with the idea that indels in repeated
   contexts are harder for dbSNP itself to deduplicate administratively,
   not just for our comparison script.
3. The 85% match rate (vs 99.7% for SNVs) confirms that the
   prefix/suffix normalization limitation is real and non-negligible, but
   does not prevent obtaining a usable result on the majority of indels.
