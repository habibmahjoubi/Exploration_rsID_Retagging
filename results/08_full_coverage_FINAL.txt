A gap of ~26 points -- precisely confirms the bias anticipated and documented from
the start: sorting by rsID number over-represents older, heavily-
studied variants (hence more likely to have accumulated merges over 25 years)
relative to a true random draw across the full spectrum.

CONCLUSION
----------
The corrected figure is more conservative but still substantial: nearly
one real variant in two (47.78%) sits on a dbSNP identifier that has
historically absorbed at least one merged predecessor. The paper's
qualitative conclusion (the mechanism exists at scale) is confirmed,
not invalidated -- only the precise figure changes, in the expected direction.

IMPORTANT -- what is NOT yet recomputed at this full scale:
the "downstream impact" panel (2014-2017-merge candidates, GATK/GDC bundle
tests, results 05-07) is still derived from the partial sample (315,939
records), not the full 25.9M. A recent-merge candidate panel built on
full coverage would give a more reliable candidate count and stale rate --
that is the next priority step if the paper moves forward (see project_brief.md).
