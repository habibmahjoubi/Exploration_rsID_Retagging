"Downstream impact" test #3: multi-version comparison of real bundles (138 vs 144)
==================================================================================

Goal: show a temporal TREND (not an isolated point), like Paper B's 13
Kraken2 databases, by testing the same panel of 2,788 candidates (2014-2017 merges)
against two real GRCh38 bundles of different vintages.

Bundles tested:
  - Homo_sapiens_assembly38.dbsnp138.vcf.gz (Broad, GATK best-practices, content
    dbSNP build 138, ~2014) -- already tested in results/05.
  - dbsnp_144.hg38.vcf.gz (Broad, "gdc/" path, used by the NCI Genomic Data
    Commons -- TCGA and other cancer cohorts), fileDate=20150603, dbSNP_BUILD_ID=144.

RESULT
--------
                                  dbSNP138 (2014)    dbSNP144 (June 2015)
Total tested                         2,788               2,788
Position absent from the bundle         8                  31
Current ID (up to date)             2,464 (88.4%)       2,632 (94.4%)
STALE ID                              217 (7.78%)          19 (0.68%)
Different ID (to investigate)          99                 106

=> The stale-identifier rate drops from 7.78% to 0.68% between the two bundles,
   in the expected direction (a more recent bundle has already absorbed most of the
   2014-2015 merges in this panel). This is a REAL temporal trend measured on two
   real, independently published/deployed artifacts, not a single-point artifact.

Examples stable across both versions (stale in both, merge later than
June 2015):
  chr20:47829240  current=rs440633  stale (BOTH 138 AND 144) = rs80132470 (merged 2016-07-19)
  chr20:13366886  current=rs657719  stale (BOTH 138 AND 144) = rs77717214 (merged 2015-07-02)

INTERPRETATION
--------------
This result directly reinforces the link with Paper B/KDCR: "reference
drift" is not binary (frozen = bad, up to date = good) but gradual and
measurable as a function of the bundle's age relative to the merge date -- exactly
the kind of dose-response relationship a calibrated governance model (like KDCR)
could exploit to decide WHEN to refresh a reference rather than freezing it
indefinitely or refreshing it on every change.

LIMITATIONS
-------
- Only 2 comparison points (138, 144) -- a 3rd point (151/152 or current)
  would strengthen the curve; dbSNP151/156 are only available in the same bucket
  as a proprietary Illumina Nirvana (.nsa) binary format, not standard VCF --
  would require either the Nirvana tool or another source for a real 3rd VCF point.
- Panel still restricted to the 2014-2017 candidates from the biased sample
  (old rsIDs) -- full coverage in progress (see README.md).
- The "position absent" and "different ID" categories increase slightly with the
  more recent bundle (31 and 106 vs 8 and 99) -- not yet explained, to investigate before
  presenting the table as definitive.
